How the Quiet Breakout works

Full rules
  1. 01

    Wait for calm

    Gold has to go quiet first. The candle size (ATR) must be at or below normal.

  2. 02

    Trade the breakout

    A 1-hour candle closes past the 20-hour high or low, in the direction of the 50-day trend.

  3. 03

    Trail the stop

    The stop starts 3 ATR away and follows price. There's no fixed target, so winners run. Close before 5 PM.

        Why the desk and Scott's calls don't match

        • Different methods. Scott reads the chart with his own judgement, which is his version of ICC. The desk follows fixed rules that were tested on 5 years of data. Two different methods will often disagree, and that doesn't mean either one is broken.
        • Different timing. His calls can come from 1-minute to 15-minute charts and land at any moment. The desk only acts when a 15-minute or 1-hour candle closes, so it's usually later and less frequent.
        • Different prices. Brokers quote gold a few dollars apart. The desk uses PAXG shifted to match spot gold (the gap is shown on the Gold page), so his entry might sit a little above or below the desk's numbers.
        • The fair test is results. Track his calls here for a few weeks next to the bots and your own trades. The "Who's doing best" table will show which one actually works.

        The plan: Quiet Breakout

        Held up 5 years
        1. Chart: gold, 1-hour candles. Only decide when a candle has closed.
        2. Trend: gold above its 50-day average means buys only. Below it means sells only.
        3. Calm first: the candles must be small. ATR(14) has to be at or below its usual level (its 100-hour average). Breakouts out of a jumpy market are skipped.
        4. Buy when a 1-hour candle closes above the highest high of the previous 20 candles. Sell when it closes below the lowest low.
        5. Stop: 3 × ATR away. Then it trails. Close the trade if a 1-hour close lands more than 3 × ATR back from the best close since you entered.
        6. No fixed target. Let winners run. Close before 5 PM, when gold takes its daily break, and before the Friday close.
        7. Risk 1% of your account per trade, and only one trade at a time.

        The desk works all of this out for you. The Gold page shows each check live.

        Why the old desk was sometimes off, and what changed

        • Strategies that didn't hold up. Over 5 years, ICC, RSI-2 and the 30-min Bollinger breakout lost money or only broke even. A 1-year test made them look better than they were. Now only the Quiet Breakout gives gold calls. ICC stays on the gold desk as practice. On Bitcoin, ICC lost money even with low fees, so it was replaced by Trend Rider, a 4-hour trend strategy that held up.
        • The chart and the signals used different prices. The old gold chart was TradingView's XAUUSD, while the signals came from PAXG, so arrows and levels could sit a few dollars off. Now the desk chart is drawn from the same candles the strategy reads. It's shifted by the measured PAXG to XAUUSD gap, and that gap is shown on the page.
        • The gap was measured at bad times. On weekends, PAXG keeps trading while spot gold is frozen, so the old offset could be wrong. Now the gap is measured only while gold is open, smoothed, and saved.
        • Two different ICC versions. The desk and the watcher used slightly different ICC code. Now there is one engine file. The 5-year test ran this exact file, and it gave the same results as the separate Python test.
        • Arrows were in the wrong place. The chart library only accepts whole candle numbers, so a signal time that fell between candles was drawn at the far-left edge. Arrows now snap to the candle that gave the signal.
        • Frozen data looked live. Now every page shows when the last candle closed, and it warns you if the data is late.
        • Short history. The daily 50-day average now uses real daily candles, not a few weeks of hourly ones.
